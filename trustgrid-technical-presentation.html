Problem Statement

Why existing marketplace reputation systems are inadequate

⭐
Static Star Ratings
A 5-star review from 3 years ago carries identical weight to one from yesterday. History is treated as timeless — no temporal sensitivity.
🔢
No Evidence Quality
A score from 2 transactions looks identical to one from 2,000. There is no signal for how much we should trust the score itself.
🤷
Zero Explainability
Users see a rating change but never understand why — which behaviour caused it, or what actions would improve it.
🎯
TrustGrid's goal: Model trust as a dynamic, evidence-gated score derived from observed behaviour — with temporal weighting, confidence levels, and plain-language explanations baked in.

Scoring Mathematics

Time decay · ML blending · Score smoothing · Final formula

① Exponential Time Decay (90-day half-life)
w(t) = exp(−λ × age_days)
λ = ln(2) / 90 ≈ 0.00770

w(0 days) ≈ 1.000 ← today
w(30 days) ≈ 0.794
w(90 days) ≈ 0.500 ← half-life
w(180 days) ≈ 0.250
w(270 days) ≈ 0.125 ← minimal
② ML Blending (β grows with evidence)
β = 0.0 (weight < 1) — brand-new user
β = 0.3 (weight 1–10) — early evidence
β = 0.5 (weight > 10) — established

ml_raw = 20 + P(reliable) × 80
final = β×ml_raw + (1−β)×rule_raw
③ Score Smoothing (prevents single-event swings)
new_score = α×model_score + (1−α)×old_score

α = 0.20 — LOW confidence (history dominates)
α = 0.50 — MEDIUM confidence (balanced)
α = 0.80 — HIGH confidence (model trusted)

Example: new user, first order, model=750
new = 0.20×750 + 0.80×700 = 710
④ Final Formula
RawScore = 0.25×D1 + 0.25×D2 + 0.20×D3
+ 0.15×D4 + 0.15×D5

TrustScore = clamp(round(10 × RawScore), 0, 1000)

Each Dᵢ ∈ [0,100] → RawScore ∈ [0,100]
TrustScore ∈ [0, 1000] (guaranteed)

Limitations & Future Scope

Honest academic positioning and roadmap

Current Limitations
●
Synthetic training data — XGBoost trained on simulated profiles. Real-world behaviour distributions will differ.
●
No anomaly detection — Sudden score spikes from account takeovers or coordinated fake activity are not flagged.
●
Simulated payments — Payment events use a 95% fixed success rate. No real payment processor.
●
Single-marketplace scope — Trust scores do not transfer across partner platforms.
●
No fairness audit — Demographic impact of scoring has not been formally tested.
Future Enhancements
●
Anomaly detection — Isolation Forest or LSTM-based drift detection for sudden behavioural shifts.
●
LSTM / Transformer — Sequence modelling for temporal event order, not just aggregate features.
●
Graph-based trust network — Detect fake referral rings and colluding user clusters.
●
Kafka event streaming — Real-time scoring at marketplace scale with event-driven architecture.
●
SHAP explainability — Feature importance visible in the admin dashboard per-user.
●
Cross-platform reputation — Allow trust scores to transfer between partner marketplaces.
Academic Contribution
TrustGrid demonstrates that a meaningful, dynamic trust score can be constructed from observable marketplace behaviour by integrating: (1) multi-dimensional behavioural scoring, (2) exponential time decay, (3) XGBoost reliability estimation, (4) evidence-gated confidence and privilege allocation, and (5) plain-language explainability — positioned against traditional static reputation systems that lack temporal sensitivity and behavioural depth.
